Should you replace your roof before going solar?

Updated 2026·3 min read

This is one of the most common questions homeowners ask before a solar install, and the answer comes down to one number: how many years does your current roof have left?

The rule of thumb

If your roof has fewer than 10 to 15 years of expected life remaining, most installers - including us - recommend replacing it before the solar install. Otherwise, you're likely to need a roof replacement mid-way through your system's 25-year warranty period, which means removing and reinstalling the array at additional cost.

What that removal actually involves

Taking down and reinstalling an existing array isn't part of a standard roofing job. It requires a licensed solar technician, adds labor time, and is an avoidable cost if the sequencing is planned upfront.

Why one company handling both matters

A roofer without an electrical license can't evaluate how roof work affects your solar system, and a solar installer without a home improvement license can't legally do the roofing work itself. All Season Solar holds the NJ Electrical License and the NJ HIC License in-house, so the same team can assess your roof's remaining life as part of your solar consultation and recommend the right order for your home.
