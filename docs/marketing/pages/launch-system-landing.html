What actually goes wrong

It's never the selling.

These are the seven that take people out. Not in year three — usually somewhere between month two and month six, while the first jobs are still running.

Lead flow

Your book came with the company

The first few jobs come from your phone — friends, past customers, the referral you were already sitting on. Then it goes quiet, and you find out whose name the phone was actually ringing for. Most new contractors have no idea how expensive a lead is until they buy their first one.

Cash

You pay before you get paid

Material on delivery. Crew on Friday. The homeowner pays on completion, or when the finance company funds, or when the check clears. Profitable companies run out of money in that gap constantly. It is the single most common way this ends.

Licensing

Paperwork gates the first permit

Entity, HIC registration, general liability, workers' comp, supplier accounts. None of it is hard. All of it takes longer than you think, has to be done in a particular order, and blocks you from pulling a permit until it's finished.

Crews

Your best sub is already someone's best sub

The crew you'd want is booked by the company you just left, and they are not going to risk that relationship for one unproven job. Finding, vetting, and keeping installers is a job by itself, and it doesn't wait until you're ready.

Permits

Every town is its own country

Different forms, different fees, different inspection windows, different tolerance for a contractor nobody there has heard of. You'll learn this municipality by municipality, on the clock, with a homeowner waiting.

Warranty

The callback is yours now

When you were selling, a leak in year two was somebody else's problem to resolve. Now it's a truck, a day, and a conversation you're having personally — and how you handle it decides what your reviews say about you.

The phone

You're on a roof when the lead comes in

You cannot sell and answer at the same time, and the homeowner is not waiting. They called three companies. Whoever answers first usually wins, and for the first year that person needs to be you or a system standing in for you.

Straight answers

The questions everyone asks.

Do I have to quit before we start?

No, and usually you shouldn't. Most of the foundation work happens while you're still earning. We'd rather build it around your current schedule than have you burning savings during setup.

What about my non-compete?

Take it to an employment attorney before you do anything — that's a legal question and we're not the ones to answer it. Bring us whatever they tell you and we'll plan the launch around it, including timing and geography.

What does it cost?

There's a setup fee for the build and a monthly fee once the front door is running. We'll give you both on the call, along with what you should expect to spend on ads — which is usually the bigger number and the one people forget to plan for.

Do I own what you build?

The entity, the brand, the domain, the customer list, the reviews — yours, in your name, from day one. The platform is licensed while you're a customer. Ask us to be specific about which is which on the call, and we will be.

How many of these are you taking?

A small number right now, deliberately. This is hands-on work and we'd rather do it properly for a few people than badly for a lot of them.
